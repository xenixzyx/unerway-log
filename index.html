How this is calculated

Need is set at 8.2 hrs a day (or your sleep goal if it's higher). In the biggest lab study of chronic sleep loss, mistakes piled up once people were awake more than 15.84 hrs a day, which means needing about 8.16 hrs of sleep (Van Dongen et al., 2003).

Sleep debt is every hour short of that over the last 14 days. That study found the damage grows almost in a straight line with those lost hours, and that people mostly stop feeling it after a few days.

Paying it back is slow. Extra sleep counts at half value here, and no more than 2 hrs of credit per night. One long night didn't fully undo 5 short ones (Banks et al., 2010), 3 normal nights didn't fix a week of restriction (Belenky et al., 2003), and one hour of debt took about 4 days of extra sleep to clear (Kitamura et al., 2016). The half-value rule is this app's simplification of those results, not a number from any one study.

The score: 14 nights of 6 hrs in that study hurt attention about as much as one full night without sleep, and 14 nights of 4 hrs about as much as two. That works out to about 30 hrs of debt per all-nighter. The score falls from 100 by a third for each all-nighter's worth. Being awake 24 hrs straight has been compared to a 0.10% blood alcohol level (Dawson & Reid, 1997).

Log actual sleep, not rack time. In one study, 7 hrs in bed got people about 6.3 hrs of sleep (Belenky et al., 2003). This is an estimate for an average person, not a medical test.
